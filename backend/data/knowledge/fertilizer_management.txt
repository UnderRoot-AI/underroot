Urea (46% N) is the most common nitrogen fertilizer. Apply in splits: half at sowing, half 30 days later. Avoid applying before heavy rain as it can wash away. 1 bag (50 kg) urea gives about 23 kg nitrogen. Cost-effective but must be applied carefully.

DAP (Di-Ammonium Phosphate, 18-46-0) provides both nitrogen and phosphorus. Best applied at sowing time mixed into the soil. 1 bag (50 kg) gives 9 kg N and 23 kg P2O5.

SSP (Single Super Phosphate, 16% P2O5) is a cheaper phosphorus option. Also provides sulfur and calcium. Good for oilseeds and pulses.

MOP (Muriate of Potash, 60% K2O) is the main potassium fertilizer. Apply at sowing. Potassium makes the crop strong and resistant to disease. 1 bag (50 kg) gives 30 kg K2O.

NPK mixed fertilizers (like 10-26-26 or 12-32-16) give all three nutrients together. Useful when multiple nutrients are low at the same time.

Organic compost (Gobar khad, vermicompost) improves soil health over time. Apply 5 to 10 tonnes per hectare before sowing. It is slow acting but long lasting. Builds up organic carbon and soil life.

Zinc Sulphate helps when soil zinc is low (common in Indian soils). Apply 25 kg/hectare. Zinc deficiency shows as yellowing between leaf veins in young leaves.

When nitrogen is low: use Urea or DAP. When phosphorus is low: use DAP or SSP. When potassium is low: use MOP. When all three are low: use a balanced NPK fertilizer. When soil organic matter is low: add compost.

Dose guidance (general reference only — always confirm with a soil test result): Wheat needs about 120 kg N, 60 kg P, 40 kg K per hectare. Maize needs about 150 kg N, 75 kg P, 60 kg K. Rice needs about 100 kg N, 50 kg P, 50 kg K. These are typical ranges — your actual soil test will tell you what is missing.

Never guess fertilizer dose. Use your soil test report and ask your local Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) or agriculture officer for the correct dose for your crop and field size.
