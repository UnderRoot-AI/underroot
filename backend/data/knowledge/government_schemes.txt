Government schemes for farmers in India: Several central and state government programs support farmers with soil testing, subsidized fertilizers, and crop insurance.

Soil Health Card (SHC) scheme: Free soil testing and nutrient recommendations for farmers through the Ministry of Agriculture. Farmers should visit their nearest Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) or Common Service Centre.

PM-KISAN (Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi): Direct income support of Rs 6000 per year to eligible small and marginal farmers. Register at pmkisan.gov.in or nearest agriculture office.

Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY): Crop insurance scheme covering losses from natural calamities, pests and diseases. Enroll through bank, Common Service Centre or online at pmfby.gov.in.

eNAM (National Agriculture Market): Online trading portal for agricultural commodities. Helps farmers get better price discovery. Available at enam.gov.in.

Krishi Vigyan Kendras (KVK): Agricultural research and extension centers spread across India. They provide free or subsidized soil testing, crop demonstrations, farmer training, and guidance on fertilizer use. Visit your nearest KVK for soil test reports and recommendations.

Gujarat state schemes: Gujarat government provides subsidized irrigation infrastructure, soil testing facilities through ATMA (Agricultural Technology Management Agency), and assistance under various crop-specific missions. Contact nearest Taluka Agriculture Officer for Gujarat-specific schemes.

Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana (RKVY): Provides funds for agricultural development including soil health improvement, irrigation, and post-harvest management.

National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture (NMSA): Focus on improving resource use efficiency, soil health, and water conservation. Activities include soil health management, rainwater conservation, and organic farming support.

Natural farming (Prakriti Kheti): Gujarat and some other states promote zero-budget natural farming using Jeevamrit (cow dung and urine based microbial culture) and Beejamrit (seed treatment). This improves soil biology and can reduce fertilizer costs over time.
