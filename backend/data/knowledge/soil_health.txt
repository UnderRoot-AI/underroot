Soil health improvement: The best long-term investment for any farmer is improving soil organic carbon. Target is above 0.5% organic carbon. This requires regular addition of organic matter such as compost, farmyard manure, or crop residues over several seasons.

Compost application: Well-rotted compost or farmyard manure at 4-5 tonnes per acre (or about 10 tonnes per hectare) applied before sowing significantly improves all soil parameters. It improves water holding, releases nutrients slowly, and feeds soil microbes.

Green manure: Growing Dhaincha (Sesbania bispinosa) or Cowpea and ploughing it into the soil before flowering is an effective way to add nitrogen and organic matter. This is especially useful in Kharif season before a Rabi crop.

Soil compaction: Avoid heavy machinery when soil is wet. Deep ploughing (subsoiling) every 2-3 years breaks compaction layers that restrict root growth. Compacted soil has poor aeration and water infiltration.

Waterlogging management: If soil moisture is consistently very high (above 70-80%), check drainage. Poor drainage leads to root rot, nutrient leaching, and anaerobic conditions that harm soil biology. Raise field beds or create drainage channels.

Drought management: If soil moisture is very low (below 10%), consider deficit irrigation or drip irrigation. Mulching with straw or plastic reduces evaporation. Choose drought-tolerant crops like bajra, jowar, chickpea.

Soil health monitoring: Compare soil test results between seasons to track trends. If organic carbon is consistently below 0.5% despite applying manure, the rate of addition needs to be increased or residue burning must stop.

Integrated soil management: The best results come from combining organic matter additions (compost, green manure) with carefully timed inorganic fertilizers based on soil test results. Neither alone is as effective as both together.

ICAR soil health guidelines: According to ICAR recommendations, pH should be 6.0-7.5, EC below 1.5 dS/m, organic carbon above 0.5%, and NPK levels appropriate for the target crop. Regular monitoring through a soil health card (SHC) scheme helps farmers track these values.

Soil Health Card (SHC) scheme: Government of India provides free soil testing under the Soil Health Card scheme. Farmers receive a card with nutrient status of their soil and recommended fertilizer doses. Visit your nearest KVK (Krishi Vigyan Kendra) or state agriculture department office to avail this service.
