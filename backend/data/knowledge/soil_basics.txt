Soil pH tells us if the soil is sour (acidic) or sweet (alkaline). A pH of 6 to 7.5 is good for most crops. If pH is too low (sour), the crop cannot absorb nutrients properly. Lime can be added to raise pH. If pH is too high (sweet), sulfur or organic matter can help lower it.

Electrical conductivity (EC) measures salt in the soil. If EC is very high, the roots get stressed by salt and plants grow poorly. Too much salt in the soil can be reduced by flooding with water and improving drainage.

Organic carbon (OC) is the food and life of the soil. Higher organic carbon means the soil holds more water, nutrients, and has more helpful microbes. Adding compost, crop residues (parali/straw), or green manure increases organic carbon.

Nitrogen (N) helps the plant grow green leaves and stems. Without enough nitrogen, leaves turn yellow and growth is slow. Urea and DAP are common nitrogen sources.

Phosphorus (P) helps roots grow strong and the plant flower and set grain. Without enough phosphorus, roots stay weak. DAP or SSP (Single Super Phosphate) is used.

Potassium (K) makes the plant strong against disease and drought and improves grain quality. MOP (Muriate of Potash) is a common source.

Moisture is how much water is in the soil. Too little water stresses the crop. Too much can cause root rot. Moisture should match the crop's needs at each growing stage.

Temperature affects how fast seeds germinate and roots grow. Most crops prefer soil temperatures between 15 and 30 degrees Celsius.
