Improving low nitrogen: Apply Urea (46% N) at 1 bag (45 kg) per acre, split into two applications - half at sowing and half 30 days later. DAP (Di-Ammonium Phosphate, 18:46:0) also provides nitrogen alongside phosphorus and can be used at sowing time at 1 bag per acre.

Improving low phosphorus: Apply DAP at 1 bag (50 kg) per acre at sowing time for combined N and P deficiency. If only phosphorus is low, use SSP (Single Super Phosphate) at 25 kg per acre mixed into soil before planting.

Improving low potassium: Apply MOP (Muriate of Potash, 60% K2O) at 25-30 kg per acre at sowing time. Potassium improves drought resistance, disease resistance, and grain/fruit quality.

When both nitrogen and phosphorus are deficient: DAP (18:46:0) at 1 bag per acre at sowing, plus half a bag of Urea 30 days after sowing, is the recommended treatment.

Organic carbon improvement: Apply 4-5 tonnes of well-rotted farmyard manure (Gobar Khad) or 2-3 trolley-loads per acre 2-3 weeks before sowing. Add neem cake (50 kg per acre) alongside to protect roots. Green manure crops like Dhaincha (Sesbania) or cowpea can be grown and ploughed in. Avoid burning crop residues.

Saline soil treatment (high EC): Apply Gypsum (Calcium Sulphate) at 200-400 kg per acre before sowing and irrigate to leach salts below the root zone. Ensure good drainage. Choose salt-tolerant crops like barley, sugarbeet, or cotton.

Acidic soil treatment (low pH): Apply lime (Calcium Carbonate or Quick Lime) at rates recommended by KVK based on your specific pH. Usually 1-2 tonnes per acre for moderately acidic soils. Dolomite lime also adds magnesium.

Alkaline soil treatment (high pH): Incorporate organic matter (compost, green manure). Grow acid-tolerant crops in the short term: potato, groundnut. Gypsum application can help in sodic soils.

Fertilizer safety precautions: Never apply Urea when soil is wet or just before heavy rain - it will be washed away. Apply in the evening to reduce volatilisation. Do not mix Urea with DAP or MOP before applying. Always water the field after applying solid fertilizers.

ICAR (Indian Council of Agricultural Research) recommends regular soil testing every 2-3 years to track nutrient trends and adjust fertilizer use efficiently. Soil testing helps farmers avoid both under-fertilizing (which reduces yield) and over-fertilizing (which wastes money and harms the environment).

State Department of Agriculture: Farmers can get subsidized soil testing and fertilizer advice from their nearest Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK). KVK officers provide crop-specific recommendations based on soil test values.
